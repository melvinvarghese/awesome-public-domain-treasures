# Copyright and rights

This catalogue reports primary-source evidence reviewed on the dates in its data. It is a discovery aid, not legal advice, a legal opinion or a worldwide rights clearance. An automated passing check cannot prove public-domain status.

## What the labels mean

| Label | Meaning and scope |
| --- | --- |
| CC0 | The linked institution applies a CC0 dedication to the relevant asset. A waiver operates only as far as the contributor's rights and applicable law allow. |
| Public Domain | The primary source explicitly identifies the relevant work or reproduction as public domain, including through the Public Domain Mark. This reports its assessment; it is not a universal guarantee. |
| Public Domain — United States | The evidence supports the linked edition or asset's status in the United States. Check the law where you download, publish or distribute it. |

CC BY, CC BY-SA and other open licenses retain copyright conditions and are not public-domain dedications. Online access, institutional hosting, age and a download button do not independently establish status. “No known restrictions” can express incomplete knowledge; the main list requires stronger supporting evidence.

## Separate layers of rights

A historical original, modern scan or photograph, translation, edited edition, annotation, musical composition, performance and sound recording may have different rights. The entry describes only the version its evidence supports. A score entry does not clear a modern recording. A public-domain sculpture does not automatically clear every photograph. Item metadata may be openly licensed even when the item image is restricted.

The Met entries use item-level Open Access eligibility together with the museum's CC0 image policy. Project Gutenberg entries, where included, concern the identified text or edition in the United States; its name, trademark and license wrapper have separate terms. Check the included ebook notice rather than assuming all Gutenberg titles have the same status.

## Responsible reuse

Open the current item record and its rights statement before downloading. Check that the file you use is the asset identified by the evidence. Save a citation and a dated copy of the statement for your own records. Follow applicable jurisdictional law and examine possible privacy, publicity, trademark and contractual issues. Respect culturally sensitive material, community expectations and provenance; an absence of copyright does not answer every ethical question. Credit the creator, institution, stable record and rights statement even where attribution is not legally required. Do not suggest institutional endorsement.
